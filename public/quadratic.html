🎯Quadratic Equations: Solving ax² + bx + c = 0

Solving by Factoring

The Question

A ball's height follows a rule that equals zero at exactly two moments — when it's launched and when it lands. If those moments are the solutions to x² − 5x + 6 = 0, when does the ball leave the ground and when does it land?

The Idea

A quadratic equation usually has two answers for x. One way to solve it is to find two numbers that multiply to c and add up to b, then split the equation into two simple ones.

In one line: find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.

Watch It Solved

x² − 5x + 6 = 0

find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to −5 → −2 and −3

(x − 2)(x − 3) = 0

either part can be 0

x − 2 = 0   or   x − 3 = 0

x = 2   or   x = 3

Watch Out

A common mistake is finding two numbers that multiply correctly but forgetting to check they also add up right — 1 and 6 multiply to 6, but they add to 7, not −5. Both conditions have to match.

Why It Matters

Quadratics show up whenever something speeds up or slows down — thrown balls, braking cars, business profit curves.

The Quadratic Formula

The Question

A different ball's motion follows 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0, but this time no obvious pair of numbers multiplies and adds up right for easy factoring. Is there a formula that always works, no matter the numbers?

The Idea

The quadratic formula solves ANY equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0, even when factoring is hard: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ (2a).

In one line: plug a, b, c into x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ (2a).

Watch It Solved

2x² − 7x + 3 = 0 → a = 2, b = −7, c = 3

find the discriminant first → b² − 4ac

(−7)² − 4(2)(3) = 49 − 24 = 25

√25 = 5

x = (7 ± 5) ÷ (2 × 2)

x = 3   or   x = 0.5

Watch Out

A common mistake is forgetting the ± and only computing one answer, or mixing up the sign of b — since b is −7 here, −b becomes +7, not −7. Write out each piece (a, b, c) before plugging in.

Why It Matters

The quadratic formula always works, even when factoring doesn't — it's the reliable backup for any quadratic equation you'll ever meet.
