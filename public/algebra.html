🧮Basic Algebra: Solving for x

Solving ax + b = c

The Question

You buy 3 identical notebooks, use a 5,000 so'm discount coupon, and pay 10,000 so'm total. How much was one notebook before the discount?

The Idea

To solve for x, get all the x terms on one side and all the plain numbers on the other. When a term crosses the equals sign, it changes to the opposite sign (+ becomes −, and − becomes +). Once only the x term is left, divide by the number in front of x.

In one line: move it across, flip the sign, then divide.

Watch It Solved

3x − 5 = 10

move −5 to the other side → it becomes +5

3x = 10 + 5

3x = 15

divide both sides by 3

x = 15 / 3

x = 5 (so'm, in thousands)

Watch Out

The #1 mistake is forgetting to flip the sign — writing 3x = 10 − 5 instead of 3x = 10 + 5. The moment a term crosses the equals sign, its sign flips.

Why It Matters

This move is behind almost every word problem — splitting bills, adjusting prices, figuring out how many of something you can afford.

Variables on Both Sides

The Question

You have 2 bags with x marbles each, plus 3 loose marbles. Your friend has 1 bag with x marbles, plus 7 loose marbles. You both have the exact same total. How many marbles are in each bag?

The Idea

When x appears on both sides, first move the smaller x term across so there's only one x term left — then solve like before.

In one line: get all the x's on one side first, then solve as usual.

Watch It Solved

2x + 3 = x + 7

move x to the left → subtract x from both sides

2x − x + 3 = 7

x + 3 = 7

move +3 to the other side → it becomes −3

x = 7 − 3

x = 4

Watch Out

A common mistake is only moving the numbers and forgetting the x term is still on both sides — you have to clear the x from one side first, before you touch the plain numbers.

Why It Matters

Comparing two equal totals like this is exactly how you solve "break-even" problems — like figuring out when two phone plans cost the same.
