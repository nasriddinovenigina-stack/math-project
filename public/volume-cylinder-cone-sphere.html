Volume of a Cylinder, Cone & Sphere

The Question

A cylindrical water tank has a base radius of 3 meters and a height of 5 meters. How much water can it hold (use π ≈ 3.14)?

The Idea

For a cylinder, volume = πr²h. For a cone, volume = ⅓πr²h — exactly a third of a cylinder with the same base and height. For a sphere, volume = &frac43;πr³.

In one line: a cone always holds exactly a third of what the matching cylinder holds.

Watch It Solved

r = 3, h = 5

V = π × 3² × 5

V = 3.14 × 9 × 5

V = 141.3 m³

Watch Out

A common mistake with cones is forgetting the ⅓ factor entirely, or applying it to a sphere instead — each shape has its own formula, so double-check which one the problem is actually asking about.

Why It Matters

These formulas describe real containers and objects — tanks, silos, ice cream cones, balls — anywhere you need to know how much a curved 3D shape holds.
