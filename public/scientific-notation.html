🔬Scientific Notation

The Question

The distance from Earth to the Sun is about 150,000,000 kilometers. Writing all those zeros is a pain — is there a shorter way?

The Idea

Scientific notation writes a number as a × 10^n, where a is between 1 and 10. Count how many places you move the decimal point to get a single-digit number in front — that count becomes the exponent n.

In one line: move the decimal until one digit is left in front, and count the jumps — that's your exponent.

Watch It Solved

150,000,000

move the decimal point left until only one digit remains in front → 1.5

count the jumps: 150,000,000 has 8 jumps to get to 1.5

150,000,000 = 1.5 × 10^8

Watch Out

A common mistake is miscounting the number of decimal places, especially with trailing zeros — always count carefully one digit at a time, or check by multiplying your answer back out to make sure it matches the original number.

Why It Matters

Scientific notation makes huge numbers (distances in space) and tiny numbers (sizes of atoms) manageable to write and compare — it's used constantly in physics, chemistry, and astronomy.
