Fractions: Adding Fractions

Adding Fractions

You eat 1/4 of a pizza, then 1/6 of another pizza. How much pizza did you eat in total?

To add fractions, they first need the same bottom number (denominator). Then just add the top numbers.

In one line: same bottom number first, then add the tops.

1/4 + 1/6

give both fractions the same denominator → 12

1/4 = 3/12

1/6 = 2/12

3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12

The most common mistake is adding the denominators too — 1/4 + 1/6 is NOT 2/10. You can only add the top numbers once the bottom numbers match.

Fractions come up anywhere something is split into parts — recipes, time, money, sharing.

Subtracting Fractions

You have 5/6 of a bottle of water left. You drink 1/3 of the original bottle. How much water is left?

Subtracting fractions works the same way as adding — get a common denominator first, then subtract the top numbers.

In one line: same bottom number first, then subtract the tops.

5/6 − 1/3

give both fractions the same denominator → 6

1/3 = 2/6

5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2

A common mistake is subtracting the denominators too — 5/6 − 1/3 is NOT 4/3. Only subtract the top numbers once the bottom numbers match.

Subtracting fractions tells you how much is left after using part of something — leftover ingredients, remaining time, unused budget.
