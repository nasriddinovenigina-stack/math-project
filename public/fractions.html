🍕Fractions: Add, Subtract, Multiply & Divide

Adding Fractions

The Question

You eat 14 of a pizza, then 16 of another pizza. How much pizza did you eat in total?

The Idea

To add fractions, they first need the same bottom number (denominator). Then just add the top numbers.

In one line: same bottom number first, then add the tops.

Watch It Solved

14 + 16

give both fractions the same denominator → 12 (a number both 4 and 6 divide into)

multiply top and bottom by the same number — that keeps the fraction's value the same

14 × 33 = 312

16 × 22 = 212

312 + 212 = 512

Watch Out

The most common mistake is adding the denominators too — 14 + 16 is NOT 210. You can only add the top numbers once the bottom numbers match.

Why It Matters

Fractions come up anywhere something is split into parts — recipes, time, money, sharing.

Subtracting Fractions

The Question

You have 56 of a bottle of water left. You drink 13 of the original bottle. How much water is left?

The Idea

Subtracting fractions works the same way as adding — get a common denominator first, then subtract the top numbers.

In one line: same bottom number first, then subtract the tops.

Watch It Solved

56 − 13

give both fractions the same denominator → 6 (a number both 6 and 3 divide into)

56 already has denominator 6, so only the second fraction needs to change

13 × 22 = 26

56 − 26 = 36 = 12

Watch Out

A common mistake is subtracting the denominators too — 56 − 13 is NOT 43. Only subtract the top numbers once the bottom numbers match.

Why It Matters

Subtracting fractions tells you how much is left after using part of something — leftover ingredients, remaining time, unused budget.

Multiplying Fractions

The Question

A recipe needs 23 of a cup of flour, but you're making half a batch. How much flour do you need?

The Idea

To multiply fractions, multiply the top numbers together and the bottom numbers together — no common denominator needed.

In one line: multiply straight across, tops times tops, bottoms times bottoms.

Watch It Solved

23 × 12

multiply the numerators: 2 × 1 = 2

multiply the denominators: 3 × 2 = 6

26 = 13

Watch Out

A common mistake is finding a common denominator first, like you would for adding — multiplying fractions never needs that. Just multiply straight across.

Why It Matters

Multiplying fractions is how you scale a recipe, a mixture, or a measurement up or down by a fractional amount.

Dividing Fractions

The Question

You have 34 of a pizza left, and you want to split it into portions that are each 18 of a whole pizza. How many portions do you get?

The Idea

To divide by a fraction, flip the second fraction upside down (its reciprocal) and multiply instead.

In one line: keep, change, flip — keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second fraction.

Watch It Solved

34 ÷ 18

flip the second fraction and multiply → 34 × 81

244 = 6

Watch Out

A common mistake is flipping the first fraction instead of the second, or forgetting to flip at all. Only the fraction after the ÷ sign gets flipped.

Why It Matters

Dividing by a fraction tells you how many smaller pieces fit into a larger amount — portioning food, cutting fabric, splitting time.
