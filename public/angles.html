Complementary & Supplementary Angles

The Question

Two angles sit next to each other along a straight line, forming a straight angle of 180°. One of them measures 65°. What's the other one?

The Idea

Complementary angles add up to 90°, and supplementary angles add up to 180°. To find a missing angle, subtract the known angle from the total.

In one line: complementary sums to 90°, supplementary sums to 180° — subtract to find what's missing.

Watch It Solved

Two angles are supplementary, one is 65°

180° − 65°

= 115°

Watch Out

A common mistake is subtracting from 90° when the angles actually form a straight line (180°), or the reverse. Check whether the angles form a right angle (90°, complementary) or a straight line (180°, supplementary) before subtracting.

Why It Matters

Angle relationships show up in construction, design, and anywhere shapes and lines meet at a point.

Triangle Angle Sum

The Question

A triangular road sign has angles of 50° and 60°. What is the third angle?

The Idea

The three angles inside any triangle always add up to 180°. To find a missing angle, add the two known angles and subtract from 180°.

In one line: all three angles of a triangle add up to 180° — subtract the two you know.

Watch It Solved

50° + 60° = 110°

180° − 110°

= 70°

Watch Out

A common mistake is forgetting the total is 180° and instead using 90° or 360°. A triangle's three interior angles always sum to exactly 180°.

Why It Matters

The triangle angle sum rule lets you find any missing angle in a triangle, roof truss, or ramp, without measuring it directly.
