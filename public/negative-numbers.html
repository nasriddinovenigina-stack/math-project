Negative Numbers

Adding and Subtracting Negative Numbers

The Question

Your bank balance is 8,000 so'm in debt (−8,000), then a friend pays you back 3,000 so'm. What's your balance now?

The Idea

Same signs: add the numbers and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract the smaller from the bigger, and keep the sign of the bigger one.

In one line: same signs, add and keep; different signs, subtract and keep the bigger's sign.

Watch It Solved

−8 + 3

signs are different → subtract: 8 − 3 = 5

keep the sign of the bigger number (−8) → negative

= −5

Watch Out

The most common mistake is just adding 8 and 3 to get 11 and forgetting the sign entirely — always check whether the signs are the same or different first.

Why It Matters

Negative numbers show up anywhere something can go below zero — debt, temperature, elevation below sea level.

Multiplying and Dividing Negative Numbers

The Question

A submarine descends 4 meters every minute. After 3 minutes, how far below its starting depth is it?

The Idea

Same signs (both positive or both negative) give a positive result. Different signs give a negative result.

In one line: same signs make a positive, different signs make a negative.

Watch It Solved

−4 × 3

signs are different (− and +) → result is negative

4 × 3 = 12

= −12

Watch Out

A common mistake is forgetting to track the sign and just answering 12. Always decide the sign FIRST (same signs = positive, different = negative), then multiply the numbers.

Why It Matters

This rule is what makes repeated loss (like descending, cooling, or losing value) predictable and easy to calculate.
