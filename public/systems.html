🍏Systems of Equations

The Question

An apple and a banana together cost 12,000 so'm. Two apples and a banana cost 19,000 so'm. What's the price of one apple?

The Idea

A system of equations has two unknowns and two equations. Subtract one equation from the other to cancel out one variable, then solve for what's left.

In one line: line up the equations, subtract to cancel one variable, then solve for the other.

Watch It Solved

a + b = 12,000

2a + b = 19,000

subtract the first equation from the second → the b's cancel

(2a + b) − (a + b) = 19,000 − 12,000

a = 7,000

plug a back into the first equation to find b

7,000 + b = 12,000

b = 5,000

Watch Out

A common mistake is subtracting the equations in a mismatched way — not lining up the same variable's terms before subtracting. Write one equation directly under the other so matching terms are stacked, then subtract straight down.

Why It Matters

Systems of equations let you solve real problems with two unknowns at once — like finding the price of two items from combo purchases, or two unknown speeds or quantities.
