🍏Systems of Equations

The Question

2 apples and 3 bananas together cost 22,000 so'm. 4 apples and 1 banana cost 14,000 so'm. What's the price of one apple, and one banana?

The Idea

A system of equations has two unknowns and two equations. If a variable's coefficients don't already match between the two equations, multiply one whole equation by a number so they do — then add or subtract the equations to cancel that variable.

In one line: scale an equation so a variable's coefficients match, cancel it out, then solve for what's left.

Watch It Solved

2a + 3b = 22,000

4a + b = 14,000

neither variable's coefficients match yet — multiply the second equation by 3 so b matches

3 × (4a + b) = 3 × 14,000 → 12a + 3b = 42,000

now subtract the first equation → the b's cancel

(12a + 3b) − (2a + 3b) = 42,000 − 22,000

10a = 20,000

a = 2,000

plug a back into either original equation to find b

4(2,000) + b = 14,000

b = 6,000

Watch Out

A common mistake is subtracting the equations before any coefficients match — that only cancels a variable if both equations already have the exact same coefficient for it. Always scale one equation first so a variable's coefficients line up.

Why It Matters

Systems of equations let you solve real problems with two unknowns at once — like finding the price of two items from combo purchases, or two unknown speeds or quantities.
