Fundamental Counting Principle

The Question

A restaurant menu has 3 choices of soup and 4 choices of main dish. If you pick exactly one soup and one main dish, how many different meal combinations are possible?

The Idea

The Counting Principle says: if one choice can be made in m ways and a second, independent choice can be made in n ways, then together there are m × n ways to make both choices.

In one line: multiply the number of options at each step.

Watch It Solved

3 soups, 4 main dishes

multiply the number of choices at each step

3 × 4

= 12 different meal combinations

Watch Out

A common mistake is adding the choices instead of multiplying (3 + 4 = 7) — adding only works if you're choosing ONE OR the other, not one of EACH.

Why It Matters

This rule is behind counting passwords, outfit combinations, license plates — anywhere independent choices combine.
