Solving Inequalities

The Question

You have 60,000 so'm to spend on notebooks that cost 10,000 so'm each. What numbers of notebooks can you afford?

The Idea

An inequality works just like an equation — do the same thing to both sides. The one twist: if you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, the inequality sign flips direction.

In one line: solve it like an equation, but flip the sign if you multiply or divide by a negative.

Watch It Solved

10,000x ≤ 60,000

divide both sides by 10,000 (positive, so the sign stays the same)

x ≤ 60,000 ÷ 10,000

x ≤ 6

now watch what happens with a negative number: −2x > 8

divide both sides by −2 — since it's negative, the sign flips

x < −4

Watch Out

A common mistake is forgetting to flip the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative — writing x > −4 above instead of x < −4. Always flip the sign when the number you multiply or divide by is negative.

Why It Matters

Inequalities describe limits and ranges — budgets, speed limits, minimum scores — anywhere there's a boundary instead of one exact number.

Enter just the boundary number for x (for example, enter 6 for x ≤ 6).
