Reading Bar Graphs

The Question

A bar graph shows how many books each of 4 classmates read this month: Aziz read 3, Malika read 7, Bekzod read 5, and Nodira read 5. Looking at the bars, who read the most books, and how many more than Aziz?

The Idea

On a bar graph, taller bars mean bigger numbers. To compare two bars, read the value each bar reaches, then subtract to find the difference.

In one line: read the height of each bar as its value, then compare or subtract.

Watch It Solved

Aziz: 3, Malika: 7, Bekzod: 5, Nodira: 5

the tallest bar belongs to Malika (7 books)

difference from Aziz: 7 − 3

Malika read 4 more books than Aziz

Watch Out

A common mistake is reading the wrong bar or misreading the scale on the vertical axis — always check what one gridline is worth before reading a value off a bar.

Why It Matters

Bar graphs are everywhere — sales reports, weather charts, sports stats — being able to read them quickly and accurately is a practical everyday skill.
