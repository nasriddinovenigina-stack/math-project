📚Adding & Subtracting Polynomials

The Question

You're combining the cost of two shopping trips, each written as an expression: (3x + 5) and (2x − 1), where x is the price of one notebook. What's the total?

The Idea

To add or subtract polynomials, combine like terms — just like combining like terms in a simpler expression, but now with more than one term per group. When subtracting, flip the sign of every term in the second polynomial first.

In one line: line up like terms, then add or subtract each pair.

Watch It Solved

(3x + 5) + (2x − 1)

combine the x terms, then combine the plain numbers

(3x + 2x) + (5 − 1)

5x + 4

Watch Out

A common mistake when SUBTRACTING polynomials is forgetting to flip the sign of every term in the second one — (3x + 5) − (2x − 1) is NOT 3x + 5 − 2x − 1, it's 3x + 5 − 2x + 1 (the −1 becomes +1).

Why It Matters

Adding and subtracting polynomials is how you simplify totals, differences, and formulas in algebra, physics, and engineering — anywhere multiple expressions get combined.

Enter your answer like 5x + 4 (or 5x − 4 if negative).
