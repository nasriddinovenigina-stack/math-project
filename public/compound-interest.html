Compound Interest

The Question

You deposit 100,000 so'm in a savings account that pays 10% interest per year, compounded annually. How much will you have after 2 years?

The Idea

Compound interest pays interest on your interest, not just on the original amount. After t years, use A = P(1 + r)^t, where P is the starting amount, r is the interest rate as a decimal, and t is the number of years.

In one line: multiply by (1 + rate) once for every year.

Watch It Solved

P = 100,000, r = 10% = 0.10, t = 2 years

A = 100,000 × (1.10)²

year 1: 100,000 × 1.10 = 110,000

year 2: 110,000 × 1.10 = 121,000

A = 121,000 so'm

Watch Out

A common mistake is calculating simple interest instead — just adding 10% of the ORIGINAL amount every year (100,000 + 10,000 + 10,000 = 120,000). Compound interest grows a little faster because it also earns interest on the interest already added.

Why It Matters

Compound interest is how real savings accounts, investments, and loans grow (or owe) over time — understanding it is the difference between a rough estimate and the real number.
