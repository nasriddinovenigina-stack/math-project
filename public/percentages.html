Finding a Percentage of a Number

The Question

A jacket costs 50,000 so'm. It's 20% off — how much do you save?

The Idea

"Percent" means out of 100. To find a percent of a number, turn the percent into a decimal (divide by 100), then multiply.

In one line: turn the % into a decimal, then multiply.

Watch It Solved

Find 20% of 50,000

20% = 20 ÷ 100 = 0.2

0.2 × 50,000

= 10,000 so'm saved

Watch Out

A common mistake is multiplying by 20 instead of 0.2 — that gives 1,000,000, way more than the jacket even costs. If your answer is bigger than the number you started with, you forgot to convert the percent first.

Why It Matters

You'll use this constantly — discounts, tips, interest, exam scores.

Reverse Percentage: Finding the Original Price

The Question

That same jacket is now on the rack with only a sale tag: 40,000 so'm, after a 20% discount. What was the original price?

The Idea

If you know the sale price and the discount, you can work backward. The sale price is (100% − discount%) of the original, so divide by that percentage (as a decimal) to undo it.

In one line: divide the sale price by (1 − discount) to get back the original.

Watch It Solved

Sale price 40,000 so'm, discount 20%

you paid 100% − 20% = 80% of the original → 0.8

original = 40,000 ÷ 0.8

= 50,000 so'm

Watch Out

A common mistake is adding 20% back onto the sale price (40,000 × 1.2 = 48,000) — that's wrong, because 20% of the ORIGINAL price isn't the same as 20% of the sale price. Always divide by (1 − discount), don't multiply the sale price back up.

Why It Matters

This is how you check whether a "sale price" is actually a good deal, or work out a price before tax or tip was added.
