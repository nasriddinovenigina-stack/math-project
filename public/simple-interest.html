🏦Simple Interest

The Question

You put 200,000 so'm in a savings account that pays 5% simple interest per year. How much interest will you earn after 3 years?

The Idea

Simple interest is found with I = P × r × t, where P is the amount you start with (principal), r is the interest rate as a decimal, and t is the time in years.

In one line: interest = principal × rate × time.

Watch It Solved

I = P × r × t

P = 200,000, r = 5% = 0.05, t = 3

I = 200,000 × 0.05 × 3

I = 30,000 so'm

Watch Out

A common mistake is forgetting to convert the percent rate into a decimal first — multiplying by 5 instead of 0.05 gives an answer 100 times too large. Always divide the percent by 100 before multiplying.

Why It Matters

Simple interest is the basis for understanding savings accounts, loans, and how money grows (or costs) over time.
