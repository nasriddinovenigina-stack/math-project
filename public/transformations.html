Transformations: Translations & Reflections

Translations

The Question

A dot on a video game map starts at (3, 2). The character moves the dot 4 spaces right and 5 spaces down. What are its new coordinates?

The Idea

A translation slides every point the same distance in the same direction, without rotating or resizing. To translate (x, y) by (a, b), just add: new point = (x + a, y + b).

In one line: add the shift to each coordinate — (x, y) becomes (x + a, y + b).

Watch It Solved

start: (3, 2), shift: right 4, down 5

right adds to x, down subtracts from y

new x = 3 + 4 = 7

new y = 2 − 5 = −3

new point = (7, −3)

Watch Out

A common mistake is mixing up which direction adds and which subtracts — right and up ADD to x and y, left and down SUBTRACT from x and y.

Why It Matters

Translations are how animations, game characters, and map markers move smoothly from one position to another.

Reflections

The Question

A photo editing app lets you flip an image like a mirror. If a point on the image is at (5, 3) and you flip the whole image across the x-axis, where does that point end up?

The Idea

A reflection flips a shape over a line like a mirror. Reflecting over the x-axis flips the sign of y: (x, y) → (x, −y). Reflecting over the y-axis flips the sign of x: (x, y) → (−x, y).

In one line: flipping over the x-axis flips the sign of y; flipping over the y-axis flips the sign of x.

Watch It Solved

point (5, 3), reflect over the x-axis

x-axis reflection flips the sign of y only

x stays 5, y becomes −3

new point = (5, −3)

Watch Out

A common mistake is flipping the wrong coordinate — reflecting over the x-axis changes y (not x), and reflecting over the y-axis changes x (not y).

Why It Matters

Reflections are what make symmetry work — mirror images, folded shapes, and butterfly-wing patterns all rely on this exact flip.
