🍎Combining Like Terms

The Question

You have 3 apples and 2 oranges. Your friend gives you 4 more apples and 1 more orange. How many apples and oranges do you have now?

The Idea

Like terms have the exact same variable (and exponent) — you can only combine terms that match. Add or subtract their coefficients (the numbers in front), and leave the variable part unchanged.

In one line: only combine terms with the exact same variable — add the numbers, keep the letter.

Watch It Solved

3x + 2y + 4x + 1y

group the matching terms together → x's with x's, y's with y's

(3x + 4x) + (2y + 1y)

7x + 3y

Watch Out

A common mistake is combining terms that don't actually match — like adding 3x and 2y to get 5xy. x and y are different variables and can never be combined into one term.

Why It Matters

Combining like terms is the very first cleanup step in almost every algebra problem — you can't solve an equation until it's simplified as much as possible.
